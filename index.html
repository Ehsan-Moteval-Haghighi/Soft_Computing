04

Uncertainty and QC

Find weak points before they become false confidence: leakage, missing data, bias, and shortcuts.

Example project

Geochemistry shortcut test: compared models with and without geochemistry to see if predictions came from geology or sampling artifacts.

My contribution: I questioned whether high performance came from real geological signal or from spatial sampling bias, then designed tests to separate those two explanations.

05

Evaluation

Test whether performance survives realistic conditions instead of relying on easy random splits.

Example project

Spatial validation: held out complete regions and belts to test whether model performance survives real geographic separation.

My contribution: I used spatial and leave-one-site-out validation because random splits can overstate performance when nearby samples share the same geological context.

How I think

Examples of experiment design

Sampling design for ML evaluation

In mineral prospectivity work, I did not treat sampling as a small preprocessing detail. I tested how positive and negative samples were selected, how spatial blocks were held out, and how model scores changed when validation became more realistic.

  • Question: Is the model learning geology or memorizing spatial location?
  • Experiment: Compare random splits with spatial holdout and leave-one-belt-out validation.
  • Contribution: Showed when simple scores were misleading and when predictions were more defensible.

Training data quality in PhD research

In my PhD, I studied why some neural constitutive models fail even when the architecture is powerful. I focused on whether the training data actually contains the elastic and plastic information needed for path-dependent prediction.

  • Question: What must a stress-strain dataset contain for ML to learn material behavior?
  • Experiment: Generate controlled datasets with different elastic/plastic information and compare model behavior.
  • Contribution: Shifted the focus from only model architecture to data quality and phase-space design.

Transfer learning across sites

For geoscience AI, I tested whether models trained on one geological region can help in another. The important part was not only whether transfer worked, but identifying when it failed and why.

  • Question: Which sites are useful donors for a new target area?
  • Experiment: Train on different belts, test on unseen belts, then compare hard donor selection and soft weighting.
  • Contribution: Demonstrated that more data is not always better; donor relevance and target similarity matter.

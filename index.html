Modelling across scales

From the microstructure to the continent

The same questions come up at every scale: which equations govern the system, what data constrain it, and when a learned model can replace or complement the physics.

  1. µm – mm

    Microstructure (RVE)

    Transient representative volume elements of porous and particle-reinforced solids and double-porosity soils, solved by FEM as a virtual laboratory.

  2. material point

    Constitutive behaviour

    Path-dependent plasticity, elasticity and soil-water retention learned by LSTM, GRU and thermodynamically constrained recurrent networks.

  3. m – 100 m

    Structures and soil layers

    FE² consolidation, Mandel's problem, strip footings, plates and notched panels under reversed loading; cohesive cracks in unsaturated porous media (XFEM).

  4. km

    Mineral deposits

    3D prediction of lithology, alteration, minerals and grade from drill holes, with geology-informed spatial features and few-hole transfer to new deposits.

  5. region – continent

    Landscapes and river networks

    Satellite and geophysical rasters, stream chemistry along river networks, and censored measurements for critical-mineral source mapping.

The mathematics

Equations I work with

Physics-based models, numerical methods and statistical learning, used together.

Coupled poromechanics (Biot)

$$\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}' - \alpha\,p\,\mathbf{I}\right) + \rho\,\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{0}$$
$$\alpha\,\dot{\varepsilon}_v + \tfrac{1}{M}\,\dot{p} - \nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\tfrac{\mathbf{k}}{\mu}\nabla p\right) = 0$$

Consolidation, unsaturated thermo-hydro-mechanical flow, and double porosity with two drainage time scales.

Computational homogenisation (FE²)

$$\langle \boldsymbol{\sigma} : \delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \rangle_{\Omega_{\mathrm{RVE}}} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma} : \delta\mathbf{E}$$

A microscale boundary-value problem at every Gauss point, with consistent tangents for the macroscale Newton solver.

Thermodynamic consistency

$$\mathcal{D} = \boldsymbol{\sigma} : \dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} - \dot{\psi} \;\ge\; 0$$

Neural surrogates with a learned convex free energy and a positive semi-definite dissipation operator, so the second law holds for every weight and every load history.

Learning and uncertainty

$$\theta^{*}_{\text{new}} = \arg\min_{\theta_{\text{phys}}} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathcal{L}\big(f_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{k}),\, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{k}\big)$$

Few-shot transfer that refits only the physics part of a model, plus kriging, censored (Tobit, Kaplan–Meier) and conformal methods for honest uncertainty.

Featured research, 2026

Physics-constrained neural surrogates for multiscale mechanics

Two manuscripts that connect differential equations, homogenisation and machine learning, built and verified end to end.

Manuscript · 2026

Two-scale (FE²) consolidation of micro-heterogeneous soils: a verified transient-RVE model, a thermodynamically constrained recurrent surrogate, and few-shot transfer to new microstructures

Builds and verifies a transient RVE model of Biot consolidation for double-porosity soils (sand with clay lenses), then trains a thermodynamically constrained GRU (TC-GRU) on it. The work supplied to the network is proven never to be negative, for every load history and every time-step sequence.

  • 0.27 % settlement error against direct simulation, vs 10.73 % for classical steady homogenisation
  • 0.05–0.07 % median stress error on 570 held-out histories in 17 test sets
  • Transferred to 8 new microstructures by refitting 1,296 of 238,869 parameters
  • Replaces the RVE in 27 coupled macroscale analyses, 5–21× faster than full FE²
Manuscript · 2026

Few-shot transfer of a thermodynamically consistent recurrent surrogate across microstructures, from the material point to reversed macroscale loading

A GRU trained on RVE stresses and consistent tangents, with an incremental energy head and an energy-split physics layer that makes the discrete dissipation non-negative by construction, adapted to 14 new porous and particle-reinforced cells.

  • 5-shot stress error 1.8–5.3 MPa vs 4.6–55.5 MPa zero-shot
  • 0 of 168 transferred models violate the dissipation inequality
  • ~130× fewer CPU core-hours than FE² on a plate with a hole, reversed bending and a notched panel
  • A new microstructure costs 0.11 core-hours of data and 4 GPU minutes of fine-tuning

Transfer learning

Carrying a model to where it has not seen data

Most of my work asks the same question in different domains: how little new data does a trained model need to work somewhere new, and how do you prove it?

New microstructures

Physics-constrained surrogates adapted with 5–60 simulated histories of a new cell, keeping the second law intact.

New mineral deposits

A library model trained on other deposits, adapted with only 10 or 30 logged drill holes at the target, and tested against inverse-distance weighting and kriging.

Choosing donors

Leave-one-belt-out and analogue-deposit ranking to decide which source sites help a new target and which hurt it.

New regions

Models held out by whole state, river basin or mining district, with conformal coverage restored by a small local recalibration sample.

Geoscience AI at 4Point AI

Applied modelling of the subsurface

Distance–Direction features for mineralisation

A geology-informed spatial representation: for each location, how far and in which direction the nearest logged rock type, alteration, mineral and grade class lie, learned by a neural network. On hidden drill holes it puts 3.9× more high-grade silver in the top 10 % than random, against 1.6× for ordinary kriging.

Submitted to Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence

Drill data + satellite rasters, 34 deposits

Predicting 24 geological concepts and gold in hidden ground (undrilled, between holes, below the drilling) from drill context, 541 raster features, foundation-model embeddings, TabPFN and context-set networks. Fused models match the better source without knowing which one it is.

Five hiding schemes · three service levels · deposit-grouped validation

Critical minerals from stream chemistry

Cu, Co and Mn source mapping from 78,290 public stream results, with non-detects modelled directly (Kaplan–Meier, Tobit, accelerated-failure-time, censored transformer) and a recurrent state carried down the river network. Geology ranks unseen districts at AUC 0.85 (Cu) and 0.95 (Mn).

Basin, state and district hold-outs · calibration · conformal sets

Uncertainty quantification in four levels

Geological realisations, sampling design, grade-dependent error and model-form uncertainty (Bayesian ridge, Gaussian processes, NGBoost), propagated from stochastic geometry to voxel-grade models.

500 realisations · location-aware bootstrap

A tested portfolio of ML products

Contributed to 4Point AI's technical paper series: 121 narrow products for mining, exploration and environmental services, each held out by hole, deposit, site or later period and compared with the strongest domain baseline. Forty reached a Good or Strong rating, from assay QA and drill-hole selection to learned geophysical inversion and metallurgical recovery. The clearest lesson: gains came from better data and better-posed targets more than from more complex models.

How I work

How I contribute to modelling projects

01

Data preparation

Prepare raw technical data so models can use it without hidden errors or inconsistent assumptions.

Example

Drill-hole, assay, lithology and geophysical files reviewed with traceable provenance, interval identifiers and QA outputs, so later modelling did not depend on guessing what each file meant.

02

Physics in the features

Convert domain knowledge into model inputs and architectures that represent real processes.

Example

Distance–direction geology features for ore grade, and a free-energy plus dissipation structure for material surrogates instead of a plain black box.

03

Model development

From FEM and FE² solvers to baseline ML, recurrent networks, transformers and transfer learning, compared honestly.

Example

Local, global, donor-selected and weighted transfer approaches compared across gold belts instead of trusting one model score.

04

Uncertainty and QC

Find weak points before they become false confidence: leakage, missing data, bias and shortcuts.

Example

Tested whether predictions came from geology or from where samples happened to be taken, with matched case–control sets and position-only baselines.

05

Verification and evaluation

Verify numerical models against direct simulation and test learned models under realistic hold-outs.

Example

FE² checked against fully meshed simulations; ML held out by whole region, deposit or later period, never random rows.

06

Communication

Translate experiments into papers, reports and reviewable handoffs.

Example

Journal papers, technical reports and model cards that state what data were used, how the test was run and what conclusion it supports.

PhD thesis · McMaster University · 2024

Application of deep learning in geotechnical engineering with a focus on constitutive models

Deep-learning constitutive models are only as reliable as their training data. The thesis develops and tests methods to judge the quality, completeness and consistency of stress–strain databases, covering linear and nonlinear elasticity, heterogeneity and load-path dependency, with complete, biased and distorted datasets, and checks the learned models on well-documented geotechnical boundary-value problems.
